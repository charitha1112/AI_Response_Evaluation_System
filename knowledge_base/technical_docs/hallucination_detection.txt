HALLUCINATION DETECTION IN LARGE LANGUAGE MODELS

1. What is Hallucination?

Hallucination occurs when a Large Language Model generates information that is false, fabricated, misleading, or unsupported by available evidence.

The generated response may sound confident and convincing even when the information is incorrect.

Example:

Question: Who invented the telephone?

Correct answer: Alexander Graham Bell is commonly credited with inventing the first practical telephone.

Hallucinated answer: Albert Einstein invented the telephone.

2. Why Do LLMs Generate Hallucinations?

Common reasons include:

- Insufficient training data.
- Outdated or incomplete knowledge.
- Ambiguous user questions.
- Incorrect patterns learned during training.
- Lack of reliable reference information.
- Model generating plausible text without verifying facts.
- Incorrect or irrelevant retrieved context in RAG systems.

3. Types of Hallucinations

A. Factual Hallucination:
The model generates information that contradicts established facts.

Example: Giving an incorrect historical date.

B. Fabricated Information:
The model invents names, references, statistics, events, or sources.

Example: Creating a research paper citation that does not exist.

C. Contextual Hallucination:
The response contradicts the information provided in the reference context.

D. Unsupported Claims:
The model makes claims that are not supported by the available evidence.

4. Hallucination Detection Techniques

A. Reference-Based Verification:
Compare the AI-generated response with a trusted reference answer or source document.

B. Retrieval-Augmented Verification:
Retrieve relevant information from a knowledge base and compare the generated claims with the retrieved evidence.

C. Fact Checking:
Break the response into individual factual claims and verify each claim against reliable sources.

D. Natural Language Inference (NLI):
Use an inference model to determine whether the reference context supports, contradicts, or is unrelated to a generated claim.

E. LLM-as-a-Judge:
Use an evaluation language model to inspect the response and identify potentially false or unsupported statements.

5. Hallucination in RAG Systems

Retrieval-Augmented Generation combines external knowledge retrieval with language generation.

RAG can reduce hallucinations by providing relevant evidence to the language model.

However, hallucinations can still occur when:

- The retrieved documents are irrelevant.
- The knowledge base does not contain the correct information.
- The model misunderstands the retrieved context.
- The generated answer introduces unsupported details.

6. Important Hallucination Evaluation Terms

Groundedness:
Measures whether generated statements are supported by the provided context.

Faithfulness:
Measures whether the response accurately represents the retrieved information without introducing unsupported claims.

Factual Correctness:
Measures whether the response agrees with reliable real-world information.

Evidence:
Information from trusted documents or sources used to verify a claim.

7. Hallucination Scoring

A simple project scoring scale can be:

5 - No hallucination detected; claims are well supported.
4 - Mostly supported; minor uncertainty.
3 - Some claims lack sufficient evidence.
2 - Several unsupported or questionable claims.
1 - Major factual errors or fabricated information.

The score is an evaluation judgment, not a mathematical guarantee.

8. Hallucination Detection Agent in This Project

The Hallucination Detection Agent is responsible for examining AI-generated responses and identifying false or unsupported claims.

Its responsibilities include:

- Extracting factual claims from the response.
- Comparing claims with retrieved reference context.
- Identifying contradictions.
- Identifying unsupported statements.
- Assigning a hallucination score.
- Providing evidence and explanations for its judgment.

9. Limitations of Hallucination Detection

- Reliable reference information may not be available.
- Some claims are difficult to verify automatically.
- Retrieved information may itself be incorrect.
- Different sources may disagree.
- An evaluation model may fail to recognize subtle errors.

When evidence is insufficient, the system should report uncertainty rather than automatically labeling a claim as false.

10. Conclusion

Hallucination detection is an important part of reliable AI evaluation. Combining reference-based verification, retrieval, fact checking, and language-model evaluation can help identify misleading or unsupported AI-generated information.
